Family Security Kit · Module 1

Secure the family Gmail with a passkey

Why this one first

Your family email is the recovery address for everything else — school logins, gaming accounts, the Apple or Google account itself. If someone gets into this inbox, they can reset passwords everywhere else. One strong lock here protects a dozen other accounts. That's why this is the first move, not the tenth.

What a passkey is

A passkey is a key that lives on your phone or computer and unlocks with your fingerprint, your face, or your device PIN. There's no password to steal and no code anyone can trick you into sharing. It's the strongest way to sign in that normal humans can actually use.

What you need

Your phone (or whichever device you sign into Google with most), and about 10 uninterrupted minutes.

Steps

  1. Get your phone handy. You'll approve the passkey with your fingerprint, face, or PIN — the same way you unlock the phone.
  2. Open your Google Account. Go to myaccount.google.com and sign in. This is the account everything else recovers to, so it's worth the care.
  3. Go to Security. In the left-hand menu, tap Security.
  4. Find Passkeys. Under "How you sign in to Google," tap Passkeys. If you don't see it there, open "2-Step Verification" first — Google sometimes tucks it inside.
  5. Create a passkey. Tap "Create a passkey" and follow the prompt: fingerprint, face, or device PIN. That's the whole magic trick.
  6. Check it worked. Sign out, then sign back in. Google should now offer your fingerprint or face instead of asking for a password. If it does, the hard part is done.
  7. Save your spare key. In Security → 2-Step Verification → Backup codes, generate a set of one-time codes. Print them or store them in the family password vault — not in a notes app, not in your email. This is what gets you back in if you lose the phone.

Now do the kid's account

From your kid's device (or through Google Family Link), repeat the same steps on their Google account. Their email is the recovery hub for their Roblox, school, and game accounts — same logic, smaller human.
